# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Company Analysis financial history switches per-share figures between **Split-adjusted** (default: on today's shares, so years before and after a split compare directly) and **As filed** (each year as its report gave it) with `S`. Adjusted lines are marked, hovering a year shows the other figure, and the footnote names the splits. `GET /api/security/history` returns `reported_values` beside the adjusted `values` and the company's splits in `share_basis`.
- Corporate bonds. A new **Update bonds** pipeline step (`update_bonds`) reads every bond's terms from EDINET: the shelf-registration supplements filed when bonds are priced (発行登録追補書類: amount, issue price, coupon and its reset, interest dates, maturity, first call, collateral, negative pledge, offering, and the R&I, JCR, Moody's, S&P, and Fitch ratings in the notes) and the bond schedule in each company's latest annual report (社債明細表, or the bonds-and-borrowings note for IFRS filers: issuing group company, issue date, balances, coupon, collateral, maturity). It also loads the Ministry of Finance JGB par-yield curve since 1974 and the JSDA's daily OTC reference prices (公社債店頭売買参考統計値), matched to bonds by maturity, coupon, and series. Everything lands in a new rebuildable `Bonds.db`; supplements are kept so later parser versions re-read them without downloading. A first run over the operator's data read 561 supplements and 4,948 annual reports into 6,377 bonds of 1,077 issuers, and matched 73% of public yen bonds to a JSDA quote.
- Company Analysis has a **Bonds** section: amount outstanding, average coupon and life, what falls due within a year, the latest ratings, the company's spread against bonds rated the same, a maturity ladder (the company's and its subsidiaries' bonds), its bonds plotted against the JGB curve, and a table of every bond with its ranking, coupon, dates, balance, rating, spread now and at issue, and price, linking to the bond supplement on EDINET, the annual report in the Filing Explorer, the calculator, and the bond market. Matured and redeemed bonds show on request (`H`); the sources list links every filing and downloads the stored supplement.
- Research has a **Bond market** tab (`6`): every outstanding bond of every issuer, filtered by rating group, ranking, term, industry, issuer, currency, and private placements, on a credit-curve scatter (spread against years left, coloured by rating) and in a sortable table with CSV export. Choosing a bond shows its terms, the reference price and the yield and spread it implies, its spread at issue, a peer fair value (the median spread of comparable bonds: same ranking, rated within a notch, similar tenor), whether it is cheap or rich against them, a chart of the peer spreads, the reference price history, similar bonds of other issuers, and the issuer's other bonds. **Price** (`C`) opens the Bonds & credit calculator with the bond's coupon, term, today's JGB yield, and spread filled in.
- Backtest results drill down from the portfolio to each holding: what each contributed (overall and by year), each holding's growth, yearly figures, and dividends, how the allocation drifted, and a dividend ledger with the as-paid amount and split factor of each payment (`GET /api/backtesting/result/{id}/detail`). Rolling and CSV sets keep each run's holdings and contributions (`run_holdings`): a run's panel lists them, and a new **Companies** view shows how each company did across the runs it was held in, opening to those runs. Holdings carry company names.
- A shareable, self-contained HTML report of any saved backtest (`GET /api/backtesting/report/{id}`, `?inline=true` to view): KPIs, methodology, cumulative return, drawdown, calendar-year and monthly returns, and per-holding or per-run sections that open in place, with inline SVG charts, hover readouts, tables for every value, and light and dark themes. **Report** and **Share HTML** buttons sit on every result, and archives include `report.html` and `dividend_payments.csv`.
- Keyboard shortcuts are now set per user. **Account → Keyboard shortcuts** lists every shortcut on every screen, grouped by screen and panel, with search; press **Change** and then the new key to rebind it, or **Reset** one or all. A key already used on the same screen, `Tab`, `Esc`, and browser chords are refused. Bindings are stored on the account (`GET`/`PUT`/`DELETE /api/settings/hotkeys`, new `auth.db.user_settings` table) and apply at once; key hints next to buttons and in tooltips show the user's keys. The `?` list now shows the keys of every panel and tab in view plus the global ones, and links to the editor. `G` then a letter, `/`, and `?` can be rebound too.
- Company Analysis exports: the selected financial-history metrics download as a raw-value CSV, an `Export report` action in the page header downloads a markdown report combining the company snapshot and full statement history, and `Export all filings` downloads one ZIP containing every retained filing archive for the company (each as `<doc_id>.zip` plus a `manifest.csv`).
- The web workstation now serves HTTPS on its configured port, terminating TLS itself. Startup reuses the first certificate/key pair found in `data/certs/` (`cert.pem`+`key.pem`, `fullchain.pem`+`privkey.pem`, `tls.crt`+`tls.key`, or `server.crt`+`server.key`; override the directory with `EDINET_CERT_DIR`) and, when none exists, generates a persistent self-signed pair there covering `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `::1`, and the bind host. A mismatched or unreadable existing pair stops startup with a clear error instead of being overwritten.
- Added a `check_tdnet_splits` pipeline step that captures Japanese stock-split and share-consolidation announcements from the TSE timely-disclosure service (TDnet) into a new `Tdnet_Disclosures` event table; splits are not EDINET-reportable events, and TDnet only serves a rolling ~30-day window, so scheduled runs keep the event history complete and idempotent.

### Changed

- Company Analysis sections are now Overview `1`, Financials `2`, Filings `3`, Bonds `4`, and Discussion `5` (Discussion was `4`).
- All shortcuts are declared in one place per screen and dispatched by one hook (`frontend-v2/src/hotkeys/`), replacing 35 hand-wired handlers and 12 hand-written help lists. Defaults follow one documented standard (see `frontend-v2/src/hotkeys/README.md`); two keys changed to match it: the Data pipeline runs with `R` instead of `Shift+R`, and `X` deletes selected Activity records as well as `Del`. Screening's `Ctrl+Enter`/`Ctrl+S` and Compare's `Ctrl+S` are now regular, rebindable shortcuts that still work while typing.
- New visual identity and interface language: a sun-over-ridge mark and widely tracked `SHADE RESEARCH` wordmark replace the signal ring, and the workstation moves to ink on paper with a single vermilion accent — a light navigation rail with a vermilion active tick, hairline rules instead of shadows, 2 px corners, Shippori Mincho titles, Zen Kaku Gothic New interface text, and IBM Plex Mono figures (fonts are self-hosted). Gains chart in indigo and losses in vermilion. Price, value, and backtest charts draw a thin unfilled line, and the Analysis metric strip wraps to two rows of six below 1500 px. The public homepage shows the mark in place of the product mock-up. Brand-asset links carry a `?v=` version so browsers replace cached copies of the old mark and favicon.
- `pyproject.toml` lists direct dependencies only; transitive pins moved to the generated `constraints.txt` (install with `-c constraints.txt`). The unused `patsy` dependency was removed.
- Page routes are served by the single SPA fallback instead of twenty duplicate handlers, and database bootstrap runs at server startup rather than at import.
- Route handlers no longer wrap unexpected errors in their own 500 responses; the application-wide handler logs them with a traceback and correlation id.
- Ruff now passes on the whole repository, and the coverage floor rose from 20% to 65%.
- Screening now has one set of split-event semantics across every rule shape: raw `Stock_Splits` filters match confirmed events by default and never look past the as-of date (point-in-time screens and rolling backtests can no longer see future splits), the expression join projects the latest confirmed split as of the screening date, and the rule formerly labelled `No recent split` is now `Split event` with a relative window (default 365 days anchored on the as-of date or today), an Advanced panel for confirmation status and exact cutoffs, and intent-named `+ Rule` templates. The screening API no longer returns the generated SQL (`sql_display` was removed from run responses and is stripped from cached results), keeping query internals server-side.
- The `update_stock_prices` step runs roughly 7× faster (a live 60-ticker sample took 0.43 s per ticker against 3.15 s in the last full run, which projects a 3,829-ticker refresh at about 30 minutes instead of 3 h 21 m). Per-ticker writes no longer scan the whole 15M-row `Stock_Prices` table three times to normalise blank price bases (now limited to the ticker being written), the split-adjusted read model rewrites only rows whose values change, provider requests reuse a keep-alive HTTP session per thread (a JPX request drops from ~0.8 s to ~20 ms) and are paced per provider (JPX and Yahoo at most 4 requests/s, Stooq 2/s), connections time out after 10 s instead of 30 s, and a provider that refuses every connection cools down for 5 minutes instead of 30 s so delisted tickers no longer wait through repeated Stooq timeouts.

### Security

- Saved backtests are now owned by the account that ran them: listing, result, and download routes only show the caller's own runs (older unowned runs are admin-only).
- Provider price refreshes (`/api/security/update-price`, `/api/screening/update-prices`) require the operator or admin role; the batch route takes a typed body limited to 100 tickers. The Analysis page hides the Refresh action for members.
- Administrator security settings are enforced: the saved registration mode (including invite-only), default role, access-token lifetime, and refresh idle/absolute lifetimes now apply instead of only being stored.
- Invitations are redeemed in a single transaction, so concurrent requests cannot reuse one invitation.
- Password changes and resets revoke the account's API tokens as well as its sessions; a reset token is no longer consumed by a rejected password.
- API token scopes are enforced (`*` or `read`); unknown scopes are rejected.
- Login spends the same password-hashing work for unknown accounts, so response timing no longer reveals which usernames exist.
- Request size limits now apply to chunked bodies without `Content-Length`, and responses carry CSP, frame, content-type, referrer, and (remote-only) HSTS headers.

### Fixed

- Per-share figures now read on one share basis everywhere, across splits. Multi-year averages and growth rates (the `_Rolling` tables) were computed from per-share figures as filed, so a split inside the window read as a collapse: Toyota's 5-year EPS growth to March 2026 showed −14.9% a year and is +17.4% (EPS ¥116.56 → ¥260.28 on today's shares). The rolling step now puts EPS, book value and dividends per share, per-share ratios, and share counts on the split-adjusted basis before averaging, stores each average on its own filing's basis like the figures it averages (so screens and views apply the same per-filing factor and a later split does not need a rebuild), and compounds growth over the time between the two year ends. A multi-year figure now needs a filing for each year of its window: a "5-year average" was taken from as little as one year. Company Analysis financial history, the overview's P/E, P/B, P/S, dividend yield, payout and market cap, Compare, and the Markdown report read the same adjusted figures (the overview used confirmed `Stock_Splits` rows only and ignored that issuers restate EPS for a split before filing: one 4-for-1 split showed a 7.2% yield and a 121% payout instead of 1.8% and 30%). Split detection was also corrected: `Stock_Splits` dates a split at its ex-date, so a split with a 31 March record date leaves the year-end share count and final dividend on the old shares, and it was treated as already in effect; such a split also showed up in the next year's share count as a second split (80 companies had a split counted twice, e.g. 14310 adjusted 16× instead of 4×); squeeze-out consolidations of millions to one before a delisting (6 companies) and a provider's duplicate record of one split are no longer applied; and a split between a year end and the filing of its report is now read from the report's two share counts (49 companies had one missed or misread, most from the last year). In all, 135 companies' split histories changed. A year's dividend paid partly before a split and partly after (Toyota's ¥148 for the year to March 2022: ¥120 interim before its 5-for-1 split, ¥28 final after) counts as ¥52 on today's shares, and backtests credit a dividend with the record-date rule above. Rolling backtests no longer divide market caps by the split factor a second time: screens already return split-adjusted share counts. Per-share ratios divide by the exact year-end share count before the summary table's, which a few filings give in thousands.
- Data review of the standardized tables. Six rolling metrics (operating income, pre-tax income, interest expenses, intangible assets, accounts receivable, and income-statement depreciation) and four ratios (intangibles on assets, depreciation, interest expenses, and salaries margins) named columns the statements no longer have and were empty for every filing; they now read the columns EDINET's taxonomy files those lines under (depreciation from the cash flow statement, as the income statement carries only the SG&A share), and G&A margin reads SG&A (39,102 filings instead of 353). Revenue for ratios, a new `Revenue` rolling metric, and Compare is the largest of net sales, operating revenue, and a bank's or insurer's ordinary revenue: 2,600 filings that report operating revenue only (railways, holding companies) had no margins or sales per share, a parent-only holding company's small net sales line understated its revenue, and banks had no revenue at all (their median net margin is now 11.8%). Ratio inputs that listed alternative column names summed them instead of taking the first reported. Five tickers delisted by a squeeze-out (millions of shares consolidated into one) showed prices of up to ¥16 billion: Yahoo multiplies every close before such a consolidation by its ratio. Price updates now take squeeze-outs back out of Yahoo series and repair rows already stored, and squeeze-outs no longer adjust prices in the portfolio, price charts, or the price read model. 66 filings (among them Santen Pharmaceutical's FY2017 annual report) were never added to the statements because two facts raising the same data-quality note failed the whole filing; the note is now recorded once and the filings were re-ingested. A 10-to-1 consolidation in the year of a merger (a share count down 8.07 times, not ten) is now confirmed by the stored prices (the report's P/E times EPS gives the price as traded), which found 19 more splits; the same prices show when a split taking effect the day after a year end was already restated in that year's report although no earlier report shows it (EPS and book value per share were divided by the ratio twice). Stored prices of unknown basis that still step down by a confirmed split's ratio at its date (an import made on the as-traded basis, such as a 4-for-1 split on 30 March 2026 that showed as a 76% fall) now carry an adjusted price, which screens, valuations, and the Analysis price chart read: split-sized steps left in stored prices fell from 187 of 2,257 known splits to none. A step now has to leave the price level moved: the previous test took any ordinary 7-11% day in a year for a 1.1-for-1 split and left the dividends before it unadjusted in backtests. The split verifier reads the year-end share count where the summary table's total is missing, and only annual reports; a candidate whose price did not stay moved by its ratio (an illiquid ¥20 stock ticking to ¥10 for a day, a rally) is rejected, and one that a second split in the same year explains stays pending instead of being rejected (a "Verify pending" run confirmed 2 splits and rejected 599 such moves); a candidate whose share count stayed put at the year end, at filing, and a year on is rejected too. Splits are also read from a report's own figures where share counts alone mislead: book value per share on 1/m of the year-end shares' net assets (a split before filing, in reports from before filing-date counts were given), a filing-date count 5.77 times the year-end count that the prices show was a 5-for-1 split with an issue, and any exact whole-number ratio (a count up exactly 12 times, a 15-to-1 consolidation). Which report first restated a split is read from the prices first (P/E × EPS is the year-end price on the shares the report's EPS is on), then from book value against net assets where both describe the same scope (an IFRS filer's consolidated book value beside its parent-only net assets does not). Stored prices that hand over from an older import to a provider already adjusted for a later split now carry the adjustment too (a 4-for-1 split on 30 July 2026 showed as a fall on 15 July). Pairs of consecutive reports whose per-share factors disagree with the stored prices fell from 327 to 82, each report in them holding exactly its filing's own figures (see `docs/DATA_REVIEW.md`).
- `scripts/review_share_basis.py` recomputes every 5-year average and growth rate in every rolling table whose metric is one source column and whose window is unambiguous, from the reports with per-share figures on today's shares, and compares them with the stored ones: 892,485 averages and 594,233 growth rates, 56,562 of them per-share figures with a split in the window, all match.
- The price evidence against an inferred split now takes a recorded split between the two reports it compares out by its known ratio, instead of giving no verdict: a share count up 3.96 times in 2021 that read as a 4-for-1 split (an issue of shares, as the 2017 report's price times a recorded 10-to-1 consolidation shows to within 0.4 %) had put the company's earlier per-share figures four times off; a rights issue that doubled another company's count in 2023 no longer counts as a split either.
- The standardization step corrects figures an issuer tagged a power of ten off, where two of the report's own measures agree: share counts given in thousands or a digit short, judged column by column against the count the report's profit over EPS and net assets over book value per share imply, taking the report's own figure where it gives the count right elsewhere (Laox's 9,335,103 beside 93,335,103), and P/Es within 5 % of a hundred times over (Tosho's 5,140.7 for 51.4); 11 figures in all, each checked against the report as displayed. Each is recorded with the filed value in `ShareMetrics_Corrections`, which the as-filed view shows. A share-count fall reads as a consolidation only by a whole number of shares into one, so a cancellation of treasury shares no longer shifts a delisted company's earlier reports. Adjusted closes a provider served still stepping at a split are found up to two weeks from its listed date (a 10-for-1 split listed for 30 January, with closes falling on the 21st). No stored price is left of unknown basis: 60 delisted companies are linked to their reports through their securities code and scaled to the reports' prices, three US stocks take Yahoo's closes, and exchange-rate, inflation, and index series are marked as traded (they cannot split). The older closes before 2001 were checked: against Yahoo's from 2001 they carry no dividend drift, so they were already on the split-only basis.
- Companies without a ticker in the company list (1,140 with annual reports, mostly delisted) had no split adjustment at all, as the share basis was keyed by ticker; 222 of them (BrainPad, TechnoPro, Genky Stores, …) show splits in their share counts, so their per-share history, averages, and growth read across those splits as filed (BrainPad's 3-year EPS growth to June 2022: −34.6 % a year instead of −5.6 %). They are now keyed by their EDINET code. For a recorded split, the last report before it now takes its factor from its own prices and book value where they tell: one filed before the split that already restated for it, and one filed after it that kept the old shares. A price-heuristic split record that a provider's split of the same ratio between the same two year ends explains (a March 2020 crash beside the real October split) no longer counts. `scripts/review_share_basis.py` adds an earnings check (EPS times shares over profit, independent of the P/E) and reports which price disagreements it confirms. The last 9 pending split candidates (2007–2012) were reviewed and rejected.
- Per-share figures of IFRS and US GAAP filers are now their consolidated ones. Standardization read only the Japanese GAAP summary concepts, which such a filer gives for the parent company alone, so 376 companies (Toyota, Sony, Canon, Hitachi, …) showed parent-only EPS, book value per share, P/E, ROE, and equity ratio in `ShareMetrics` and everything built on them: Toyota's EPS for March 2026 read ¥260.28 instead of ¥295.25 and its book value per share ¥1,815.72 instead of ¥3,062.82, and Sony had no EPS. The step now reads the IFRS and US GAAP summary concepts into the same columns, and a filing with any of these figures on the consolidated scope no longer takes the others from the parent company: a consolidated summary that left out the P/E (a year of ¥1m profit) had the parent's P/E beside the group's EPS (562 such P/Es are now empty). Dividends and payout stay the parent company's, as reported. The split verifier also rejects a one-day rise that only a fractional consolidation would explain (17 shares into 12; consolidations merge a whole number of shares into one): 90 limit-up rallies waiting as split candidates were rejected, leaving 9, all from before 2013.
- Stored prices before 2023 were adjusted for dividends as well as splits: the older import's closes were 0.907 of the price as traded at March 2017 (from the reports' own P/E × EPS), so historical P/E and yields were too low and backtests counted those dividends twice, once in the price and again as cash. They were replaced with Yahoo's split-only daily closes for 2,842 companies (12.8 million rows; the replaced rows and the split table are kept in `data/databases/backup_before_price_rebuild_2026-10-09.db`). Rows Yahoo cannot serve (before 2001, and 25 companies) are scaled to join the next provider close or to each annual report's price; 159 stretches where Yahoo repeats one close for months or years (Workman at ¥286.25 from 2010 to 2018) were replaced by the older closes joined at both ends. The split-adjusted read model now also adjusts provider closes that still step at a confirmed split: JPX's daily quotes fetched before a split stay on the old shares, and Yahoo listed some 2026 splits without adjusting its history (a 3-for-1 showed as a 67 % fall); 61 of 2026's splits were steps in the stored prices. A split recorded twice, by the price heuristic and the provider days or weeks apart, adjusted prices twice (one history stood at 1/25 instead of 1/5) and now counts once, there and in the portfolio and share basis. Splits inferred from share counts are checked against the prices: an issue, buyback, or cancellation of treasury shares that lands on a split ratio (a biotech's 24.98 % more shares read as 5-for-4; 239,900 of 1.2 million shares cancelled read as 5-to-4) leaves the price as traded and the stored price together, so 92 such events no longer adjust per-share figures; loss years' negative P/E and EPS now count as price evidence. A filing-date count and the recorded split it shows are one split even a few days outside the filing window, a report filed after a split took effect but still on the old shares (by its book value and its price) is no longer treated as restated, and a split restated before it took effect is no longer counted twice. With the consolidated per-share figures above, a report's P/E × EPS on today's shares matches the stored year-end price within 5 % in 98.97 % of 28,552 annual reports (median 1.000 in every year from 2016).
- Screens on the adjusted price basis (Screening and rolling backtests) compared split-adjusted prices with per-share figures as reported, so before a split every company that split later looked two to ten times cheaper on P/E, P/B, and dividend yield — a look-ahead bias that pulled future splitters into historical screens (a saved quality-and-value screen matched 20 companies on 2022-07-01 instead of 6). Per-share figures and share counts are now put on the price basis per filing, allowing for issuers restating EPS and book value for a split that takes effect before the report is filed. The benchmark in single backtests now keeps dividends as cash like the portfolio, instead of reinvesting them, so excess returns compare like with like.
- Backtest accuracy review. Dividends are now credited on the same split-adjusted share basis as the stored prices: they were taken as paid while prices are adjusted, so before a split a backtest bought the post-split number of shares and was paid the pre-split dividend on each — a Toyota 2017–2023 backtest reported a 242.9% return with 16–20% yearly dividend yields; it now reports 155.9% with about 3.5%. Splits come from confirmed `Stock_Splits` rows and from fiscal-year-end share counts in annual reports (standard ratios; near misses such as Sony's 5-for-1 with cancellations confirmed by book value per share). Each annual dividend is split into its interim and final payments at their record dates, so a holding is credited only with payments it was held for. Market-cap weights in rolling backtests put the screen's price and share count on one basis. Yearly figures now run from the previous year's close (they skipped the move across each new year), contributions use the start-of-year weights instead of the initial ones, yearly start/end prices are each year's own (they showed the whole period's), yearly dividends match what the daily series credited, the annualised return counts from the purchase day, and two benchmark payments on one day both count.
- Profile updates store the normalized username instead of the raw input.
- The "frontend build missing" 503 now shows its instructions instead of a generic error, and unhandled-error responses include the `X-Correlation-ID` header.
- EDINET document downloads build file paths with the platform separator; they previously used a Windows backslash and failed on Linux.
- The rolling backtest XLSX export no longer fails at runtime: `openpyxl` is now a declared dependency.
- The test suite no longer writes to `data/` or `config/state/` (the auth database, backtests, reports, screening history, and job workspaces are redirected to a temporary directory), and outbound network access is blocked during tests.
- Financial history no longer rounds raw values to whole numbers: Raw view renders every stored number at full precision (thousands-separated, wrapping at group boundaries), matching the granularity of the CSV export and markdown report.
- `python -m src.web_app.server` starts again instead of failing with duplicate-route registration; the module passes the already-built app object to uvicorn rather than re-importing itself by string.
- Price ingestion now rejects degenerate provider histories for every source: sparse multi-year responses (weekly/monthly bars posing as daily) fail over to the next provider, and weekend-dated rows for Japanese codes are dropped.
- Yahoo Finance backfills now send explicit `period1`/`period2` instead of `range=max` (which made Yahoo return monthly bars) and reject any payload whose `dataGranularity` is not `1d`, so full daily history lands in one request per ticker.
- JPX price fetches now call the `qjsonp.aspx` stock-detail JSON endpoint that the redesigned quote page loads client-side; the old server-rendered historical table is empty, which made every Japanese ticker fall back to Stooq/Yahoo. In-JSON error statuses are retried with backoff and honor the provider cooldown.

- Bulk price updates continue with the remaining tickers after an individual provider failure instead of aborting the run at the first failed ticker.

- Security Analysis valuation metrics now project filed per-share values and share counts onto the current-share basis for confirmed post-filing splits while preserving the filed values and exposing source-price provenance.
- Split reconciliation rollback now preserves every price-provenance column and removes any transient split rows created during a failed verification.

### Security
- Loopback is the default. Remote binding requires `--allow-remote`, account mode with an independently issued application bearer token, and `EDINET_TRUSTED_HOSTS`; `EDINET_API_TOKEN` remains provider-only.
- Added correlation IDs, one safe HTTP error envelope, request/upload/export limits, and redaction of job output and secrets.
- Database, pipeline input, upload, backtest result, and export paths are resolved against configured roots; API responses use stable database IDs instead of private absolute paths.
- Portfolio XML and embedded pipeline uploads are size-bounded, strictly decoded/validated, and owned by deterministic cleanup workspaces.

### Reliability
- The test suite is hermetic by default: generated market databases, filing archives, portfolio XML, auth/research/job state, and a minimal SPA replace operator databases and ignored local artifacts. The complete bounded verifier passes out of the box.
- Server startup now creates every missing configured Base, Standardized, Portfolio, auth, research, pipeline-job, and filings database, including managed schemas and migrations.
- XBRL acquisition reuses HTTP connections, batches `DocumentList` status updates, and caps concurrent downloads at five.
